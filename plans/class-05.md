# Class 5 — A table for two, please! 🍽️
## Teacher's Guide / Lesson Plan

### Objectives
By the end of this class, the student can:
- Request a table at a restaurant ("A table for two, please")
- Order food politely ("I'll have fish and salad, please")
- Use "please" and "thank you" naturally
- Ask for the menu and the bill
- Compliment food ("It's really good!")
- Use restaurant survival phrases independently

### Class Structure (60 minutes)

| Time | Activity | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| 0-5 min | Warm-up review | "What do you need from the supermarket?" (Class 4 review). Then: "Do you like restaurants?" |
| 5-15 min | Vocabulary | 9 new words. Focus on "I'll have" — explain it's HOW YOU ORDER in English. Drill: "I'll have [food], please." |
| 15-20 min | "Please" and "Thank you" drill | Emphasize: English uses "please" MUCH more than Portuguese. Practice adding it to everything. |
| 20-30 min | Dialogue practice — 1 & 2 | Role-play the full restaurant scene. She is Vanessa, you are the waiter (Lazaro). |
| 30-40 min | Speaking: Real restaurant order | You are the waiter. She orders her ACTUAL favorite food from her real weekend restaurant. Full sequence: arrive → order → compliment → bill. |
| 40-50 min | Dialogue practice — 3 & 4 | Focus on Dialogue 4 (the bill comedy). Ask: "Is your restaurant expensive?" |
| 50-55 min | Post-it activity | Together, decide which 10 Post-its she'll put up this week. Write the words together on camera. |
| 55-60 min | Wrap-up | Full restaurant sequence one more time: table → menu → order → "really good!" → bill. Celebration! |

### Speaking Practice Ideas
- Full restaurant simulation: entrance to exit, she does EVERYTHING
- "I'll have ___" rapid-fire: show pictures of food, she orders each one
- Politeness drill: you say a request WITHOUT "please", she adds it
- "Is it good?" → "It's really good!" / "I love it!" — enthusiasm practice
- Ask about HER real restaurant experiences this weekend

### Common Errors to Watch
- "I have" instead of "I'll have" for ordering (confusing possession with request)
- Forgetting "please" — constantly remind: "Again, but with 'please'!"
- "The conta, please" (mixing PT/EN) — drill "the bill" until automatic
- Pronunciation of "table" — PT speakers may say "tay-ble" instead of "tay-buhl"
- "Thank you" running together: should have clear "th" sound

### Self-Study Tool: Post-its at Home
- Do this TOGETHER during class (she writes, you guide via video call)
- Start with 10 food/kitchen items she learned: COFFEE, WATER, MILK, RICE, BREAD, FRUIT, CHICKEN, FISH, SALAD + one verb (I NEED on the shopping bag)
- Rule: every time she touches the object, read the Post-it out loud
- Next week: add more (verbs, adjectives)
- The "BEAUTIFUL" Post-it goes on her window with the best view!

### Key Phrases to Drill
- "A table for two, please."
- "The menu, please."
- "I'll have chicken and rice, please."
- "Water, please. Thank you!"
- "It's really good!"
- "The bill, please."

### Carry-Forward Vocabulary (use in ALL future classes)
- Please, thank you, I'll have, the menu, the bill, a table for two, really good, fish, salad
- "Please" and "thank you" must appear in EVERY future class
- "I'll have" works for any ordering situation (also shops, cafés)
- Core pattern: "I'll have [item], please." → "Thank you!"

### Milestone Check (Classes 1-5 halfway point)
After this class, student should be able to:
- ✅ Greet someone (Good morning! How are you?)
- ✅ Talk about daily routine (I wake up, I cook, I watch TV)
- ✅ Express opinions (I like/love/don't like + adjective)
- ✅ Go shopping (I need, I want, How much?)
- ✅ Order at a restaurant (I'll have..., please)
- ✅ Be polite (Please, Thank you)

If she can do all of these, she's on track! Classes 6-10 will build on these foundations.
