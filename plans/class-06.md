# Class 6 — How are you feeling? 🤒
## Teacher's Guide / Lesson Plan

### Objectives
By the end of this class, the student can:
- Describe how she feels ("I feel tired", "I feel sick")
- Name basic body parts (head, stomach, back, throat, arm)
- Describe symptoms ("My head hurts", "I have a headache")
- Communicate at the doctor's office ("I need medicine")
- Use "I have" for symptoms (I have a headache/stomachache)
- Ask and answer "How are you feeling?"

### Class Structure (60 minutes)

| Time | Activity | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| 0-5 min | Warm-up review | "Order me food at a restaurant!" (Class 5 review). Then: "How are you today? Are you tired?" |
| 5-15 min | Vocabulary | 9 new words. Focus on body parts — point to each one. Use TPR (Total Physical Response): say the word, she points. |
| 15-20 min | "My ___ hurts" drill | Point-and-say: you point to a body part, she says "My [part] hurts." Then reverse — she points, you model. |
| 20-30 min | Dialogue practice — 1 & 2 | Role-play: she is Cleci (calling Marina about feeling sick). Then switch — she is Cleci at the doctor. |
| 30-40 min | Speaking: Real health scenario | Ask: "When you go to the doctor, what do you say?" Practice a full visit: describe symptoms → doctor asks questions → get advice. |
| 40-50 min | Dialogue practice — 3 & 4 | Focus on Dialogue 4 (Cleci feels better). Ask: "What makes YOU feel better when you're sick?" |
| 50-55 min | YouTube channel exploration | Show her 2-3 beginner YouTube channels live. Pick ONE together for her to watch this week. Subscribe together. |
| 55-60 min | Wrap-up | Full doctor visit sequence one more time. Celebration — she's over halfway! |

### Speaking Practice Ideas
- Body part TPR game: you say the part, she touches it (fast!)
- "What hurts?" charades: act out symptoms, she describes in English
- Doctor role-play: full visit from "Good morning, doctor" to "Thank you, goodbye"
- Real life: "Tell me about the last time you felt sick — in English!"
- Express sympathy: teach "Oh no!", "I'm sorry!", "Get well soon!"

### Common Errors to Watch
- "I am sick" vs "I feel sick" — both correct, but "I feel" is more versatile
- "My head is hurting" instead of "My head hurts" — keep it simple
- "I have pain in the head" → "I have a headache" (teach as chunk)
- Pronunciation of "stomach" — /ˈstʌmək/ not "sto-mah-chee"
- "Medicine" pronunciation — /ˈmedɪsɪn/ (3 syllables, not 4)
- "Ache" pronunciation — /eɪk/ (sounds like "ake", not "ah-chee")

### Self-Study Tool: YouTube Channels for Beginners
- Together in class, open YouTube and show her:
  - **BBC Learning English** — short, clear, British accent
  - **English with Lucy** — young, fun, modern topics
  - **Rachel's English** — pronunciation focus
- Pick ONE channel together. Subscribe to it on her phone/tablet.
- Goal: watch ONE short video (3-5 min) per day this week
- Tip: turn on English subtitles!

### Key Phrases to Drill
- "How are you feeling?" / "I feel tired."
- "My head hurts." / "My stomach hurts."
- "I have a headache." / "I have a stomachache."
- "I need medicine." / "I need a doctor."
- "Are you okay?" / "I feel better now."
- "Take this medicine." / "Rest, please."

### Carry-Forward Vocabulary (use in ALL future classes)
- I feel, tired, sick, better, hurts, medicine, doctor, head, stomach
- "How are you feeling?" works as a daily greeting variation
- Body parts can be reused for describing people (Class 7)
- "I feel + adjective" pattern is extremely versatile for emotions

### Progress Check (after Class 6)
Student should now be able to:
- ✅ Everything from Classes 1-5
- ✅ Describe how she feels (I feel tired/sick/better)
- ✅ Name body parts (head, stomach, back, throat, arm)
- ✅ Describe symptoms (My head hurts / I have a headache)
- ✅ Communicate at a doctor's office (basic level)
- ✅ Use a YouTube channel for self-study
